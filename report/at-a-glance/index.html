Section 03

Foreword

What We're Seeing

Matt Gorniak, CEO, Threekit

satusContent-driven report shellThree intensity 1

For decades, the way enterprise manufa c turers sold complex products was simple. A buyer found a brand. The brand handed them a catalog. The catalog reached a dealer. The dealer closed the sale. That model still works in some places. It works less well almost everywhere else. The buyer who arrives on a manufacturer's website today has already done much of what a dealer used to do. They've researched alternatives, narrowed options, and looked for

prices. What they haven't done is fill out a form and wait three days for a callback. Across this audit of 100 enterprise manufacturers, the gap between what buyers expect online and what manufacturers actually deliver is the most c onsistent finding. The median Buyer Enablement Score across the cohort is 2.25 out of 10. The harder question is how manufacturers do more on the website without disintermediating the channels that still close most of the

revenue. A few sites in this audit point to a working pattern. The website does the early work. The channel still closes the deal. We hope this report is useful to the manufacturers in our cohort and to the broader category. F O R E W O R D What We're Seeing Matt Gorniak C E O, T h r e e k i t P. 0 3

Quote-form friction was determined by inline-form analysis on the contact page for a verified subset and

rules-based classification for the remainder, derived from observed primary buying path and CTA structure. Component definitions and weights are listed in section 02. We selected 100 of the largest and most recognizable configurable-product manufacturing companies in North America, intentionally weighted toward companies with non-trivial channel structures (dealers, distributors,

reps) and configurable physical products. The cohort was identified through a combination of public revenue rankings, trade-association member lists, and category coverage across the verticals where configurable selling is most material. The cohort is heterogeneous across company size, public/private status, and family ownership. It is not a probability sample of all configurable-product manufacturers; findings

generalize to the cohort, not to the universe. Doors & Windows (34 in cohort, 26 audited), Kitchen & Bath / Plumbing (23 / 21), Powersports (8 / 7), Cabinets & Closets (7 / 7 — vertical complete), Heavy Machinery (7 / 5), Foodservice Equipment (6 / 5), Medical & Dental Equipment (5/ 4), Contract Furniture (4 / 2), Custom Uniforms / PPE (3 / 3 — vertical complete), Funeral & Memorial (1 / 1), Flooring/Surfaces (1 / 1),

and one unclassified entry. 84 of 100 sites yielded usable audit data; 16 returned bot-blocks, JS-rendered shells without static content, redirects to non- buyer-facing destinations, or expired SSL certificates and are excluded from quantitative findings. Each audited site was scored on: (1) Configurator presence — interactive product visualization or build-your-own tool, scored Yes / Partial / No;

(2) Pricing visibility — whether at least one product price is displayed without form submission, scored Public / Partial / Gated; (3) Path-to-price — clicks from homepage to a dollar figure, derived from pricing visibility; (4) Primary buying path — Direct purchase / Hybrid / Contact sales / Dealer-only / Corporate-only; (5) Quote-form friction — LOW / MED / HIGH / NONE based on form depth, field count, and

accessibility; (6) AI features visible on homepage — Live AI / Mentioned only / None; (7) Guided selling flow — quiz, finder, or step-by-step tool, Yes / No; (8) Live chat or AI assistant; (9) Customer logos count — named customer references; (10) Lead-time disclosure on product pages.
